    Prototype

    Product case study · 0→1 · AI agents × UPI

    74% would hand an AI agent their shopping tasks. Only 9% would let it pay on its own.

    Rekha (रेखा, "line") is a concept for the missing layer in India's agent payments. You draw boundaries in plain language. Every payment an agent makes is checked against what you actually asked for. Anything outside the line is held for your OK or stopped, with a reason you can read in two seconds.

    Problem
    A spending cap knows how much an agent may spend. It can't tell what the agent bought or who got paid.
    Idea
    Check every agent payment against what the user actually asked for, inside the UPI app they already trust.
    What I made
    Research, a PRD with a 19-rule decision engine, metrics and experiments, a threat model, and a working prototype.
    Status
    A concept. User interviews and usability tests come next; the plan is in section 14.

    01Problem

    A spending cap knows how much. It doesn't know what.

    AI agents can already pay over UPI. Since June 2026, Pine Labs' P3P lets an agent pay after one upfront approval, and Razorpay and NPCI run in-chat checkout pilots on ChatGPT and Claude. NPCI has been preparing a Unified Agent Protocol so agents can make small payments without asking each time, within limits set in advance. Its launch was reported paused in September 2026 while NPCI waits for regulatory clearance. It is reported to build on UPI Circle, which caps delegated spending at ₹15,000 a month and ₹5,000 a payment.

    A cap limits how much an agent can lose. It can't tell a weekly grocery order from a gift box a web page talked the agent into. Research on web agents found prompt-injection attacks partially succeeded in 86% of cases. A manipulated agent can stay well inside the cap.

    When I hand a routine purchase to an AI agent, I want to set exactly how far it can go, so I can stop thinking about it without worrying that it will overspend, be tricked, or buy the wrong thing.

    05Insights

    Four ideas that shaped every decision.

    The risk isn't how much. It's what.

    Caps limit damage. Matching each payment to the user's intent prevents it.

    From: prompt-injection research; UPI Circle design

    The agent shouldn't mark its own homework.

    The platform that earns from a completed purchase can't be the only judge of whether it was right.

    From: teardown of in-chat checkout

    A constant loop isn't a human in the loop.

    Ask about everything and people approve without reading. Ask rarely and clearly, and the human is a real safeguard again.

    From: MFA-fatigue attacks in security

    A promise of guardrails isn't enough.

    Accenture's 9% were already offered guardrails they set themselves. What moves people is experience: 31% say trying low-cost, low-risk purchases would make them more comfortable. So Rekha starts small and lets agents earn autonomy.

    From: Accenture, Talk to my AI agent, 2026

    06The product

    Draw, check, ask, recover.

    Rekha lives inside the UPI app, where the user already links agents and enters their PIN. It never shops and never moves money. It decides whether each agent payment is inside the line.

    Draw

    Describe the boundary in your own words, in English or Hinglish. Rekha turns it into a structured card you confirm.

    "har hafte 2000 tak grocery, daaru nahi"
    Check

    Every agent payment runs through 19 rules in under 300 ms: shop, payee, items, quantities, prices, budget, manipulation signals.

    ALLOW · ASK · BLOCK
    Ask

    When something's unusual, the approval leads with the reason, then shows the cart diff. Declining is as easy as approving.

    "Not groceries: air fryer, ₹3,999"
    Recover

    One tap pauses every agent. Every receipt explains itself, and "report a problem" builds a signed evidence pack for the bank.

    sha256 · hash-chained log

    Autonomy ladder · users choose, and agents earn their way up

    L0Ask every timeEvery new agent starts here
    L1Ask when unusualSuggested when you set up your own Rekha
    L2Pay, then tell meGood for known billers
    L3Weekly summary onlyEarned through the Trust Ramp

    AI principle

    Models suggest. Rules decide.

    An LLM turns your words into a draft policy, which you confirm field by field. It never decides a payment. Decisions are deterministic, replayable and fast enough for UPI.

    Asymmetric error budget

    • Looser than you said is a safety incident: ≤ 0.5% on a 600-utterance eval set.
    • Tighter than you said is an annoyance: ≤ 5%.
    • Two annotators, κ ≥ 0.8; every prompt or model change re-runs the suite.
    ✕ Block: hard lines
    • R14Payee doesn't match the shop the agent was using (strongest injection signal)
    • R05Payee is a person, not a business
    • R07Cart contains something you excluded
    • R06Shop isn't in this Rekha (catches lookalikes)
    • R10Above UPI's agent limits
    • R03Kill switch is on
    ! Ask: worth a look
    • R11Intent Lock: cart doesn't match what you asked for
    • R12Quantity is 3× or more your usual
    • R13Price is 1.5× or more the usual
    • R18A repeat of something already paid today, by any agent
    • R15First payment to a new payee above ₹2,000
    • R09Over the Rekha's budget

    Full table of 19 rules with thresholds: PRD §8.

    The prototype runs these rules live. Try a payee swap, a hidden-text injection or a lookalike shop, and watch the trace.

    07How it works

    One call in the payment path, a 300 ms budget.

    Fail closed, selectively

    If Rekha is down: new payees and amounts over ₹1,000 wait for the user; known billers under ₹1,000 fall back to the rail's own limits plus a notification.

    Idempotent and replayable

    Retries return the original decision. Every decision is logged with its inputs and rule versions, so a dispute can replay it exactly.

    Privacy by design (DPDP)

    Line items kept 90 days, then aggregated. No browsing history. Unlinking an agent withdraws consent and deletes what the law doesn't require.

    08Prioritisation

    RICE is an input, not a verdict.

    Scored for a 50,000-user pilot over one quarter. Then I overrode it twice, on purpose.

    FeatureReachImpactConf.EffortRICERelease
    Override 1

    Injection Shield goes in the MVP, despite ranking 7th.

    RICE measures expected value, not tail risk. One public story about an agent tricked into paying a scammer could get the pilot paused and set back the whole category. Safety is a launch gate.

    Override 2

    A basic evidence pack ships, despite ranking last.

    The partner bank needs a dispute trail before it will agree to a pilot. It's a launch dependency, not a feature competing for users.

    Deferred

    Trust Ramp waits for P1.

    It needs weeks of clean-payment history per user before it can trigger, so building it in month one is wasted effort.

    09Metrics

    A North Star that can't be gamed by loosening the rules.

    North Star

    Trusted Autonomous Payments per week

    Agent payments completed inside a Rekha without a step-up, and not disputed, reported or reversed within 7 days.

    Why not…

    • Agent GMV: rewards bigger, riskier payments.
    • Rekhas created: vanity; creation isn't use.
    • Blocks: perverse; the team wins by blocking more.
    • % auto-allowed: gameable by loosening rules. The 7-day dispute filter closes that loophole.
    • Trusted Autonomous Payments / week
      • Breadth: users with an active Rekha
        • Agent link rate 15%
        • Rekha created in week 1 ≥ 35%
        • Time to first Rekha < 90 s
      • Frequency: agent payments per user per week
        • Agents at L1 or above ≥ 40%
        • Trust Ramp acceptance
      • Quality: allowed and not disputed
        • Auto-allow rate ≥ 60%
        • Needless asks (approved unchanged) ≤ 35%
        • False-block rate ≤ 2%

    20-case red-team suite

    Runs on every release. Gate: 19 of 20 must pass, with zero misses on any BLOCK case. 11 of the cases run live in the prototype.

    What Rekha can't solve

    A user who approves a bad request. Attacks that stay fully inside a Rekha (damage is bounded by its budget). A stolen phone plus a known PIN.

    Incident playbook

    SEV-1 pauses an agent platform-wide and notifies NPCI and the bank. Blameless post-mortem in 5 days, and a new red-team case for every incident.

    13Launch & roadmap

    Shadow first. Never launch in Diwali week.
